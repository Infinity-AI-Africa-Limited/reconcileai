## Extracted slide-safe evidence

1. The IMF’s 2025 SSA digital-payments paper identifies mobile money and fast-payment
   systems as central to the region’s payment landscape and calls for an enabling,
   competitive, interoperable, and secure operating environment. This supports a
   market narrative centred on growing payment complexity and control requirements,
   not an unsourced operational-loss figure.  
   Source: https://www.imf.org/en/publications/departmental-papers-policy-papers/issues/2025/06/27/digital-payment-innovations-in-sub-saharan-africa-529198

2. GSMA reports that mobile-money services processed more than US$2 trillion globally
   in 2025, with 2.3 billion registered accounts and 593 million active 30-day
   accounts. It also reports that merchant payments grew almost 50% to US$155 billion
   in 2025, the fastest-growing mobile-money use case. This is global context, not a
   Nigeria-only figure.  
   Source: https://www.gsma.com/sotir/

3. GSMA states that most new registered and active mobile-money accounts came from
   Sub-Saharan Africa in 2025, while interoperability, KYC, and consumer-protection
   regulation were regarded by more than 60% of providers as supportive to operations.
   This supports the deck’s governance-and-operational-control framing.  
   Source: https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/mobile-money-accounted-for-2-trillion-in-transactions-in-2025-doubling-since-2021-as-active-accounts-continue-to-grow/

4. BCG projects that African fintech revenues could expand roughly 13x to about
   US$65 billion by 2030, and describes payments as the principal engine of the
   region’s fintech growth. It also argues that the next phase requires deeper B2B and
   public-sector payment digitisation, interoperable infrastructure, regulatory clarity,
   trust, and resilience. Use this explicitly as a BCG projection rather than a
   realised-market fact.  
   Source: https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/beyond-payments-unlocking-africas-second-fintech-wave
